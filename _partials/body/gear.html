What to buy, in what order, and what to look for

You start with things you already own — a floor, a wall, a chair, a table — and that genuinely works for about four weeks. After that, one specific purchase — a pull-up bar — unlocks more than everything else combined. Below is the full list in priority order, with price ranges, what to check before buying, and what to skip.

About the prices and links. The ranges below are typical US street prices as of 2026 and move around constantly — treat them as ballpark, not quotes. The links are Amazon search links for the product category, not endorsements of a specific brand or seller. Read recent reviews, check the stated weight rating, and confirm the measurements against your own doorway or ceiling before you buy anything that holds you off the ground. The same items are usually available at Walmart, Target, Dick's, and Rogue — Amazon is just the easiest single place to compare.

Tier 1 — the $80 unlock (buy in weeks 1–4)

This is the highest-return money you will spend on fitness, probably ever. In priority order:

#ItemTypical priceWhyWhat to look for
1 Doorway pull-up bar

Leverage-mount (over-the-door frame) type, 300 lb rating, multiple grip positions including neutral, and padded foam where it contacts the frame. Full comparison below.

search →		 $20–35 Assisted pull-ups, band pull-aparts, face pulls, Pallof presses, external rotations. The shoulder-health work in every warm-up needs one. A set of 3–5 continuous loop bands of different thicknesses (not the tube-with-handles kind). Get at least one heavy band for pull-up assistance and one light band for warm-ups.
3 Exercise mat
search →		 $20–30 Saves your spine on leg raises and hollow holds, your knees on split squats, and your elbows on planks. Also defines a "training space," which matters more psychologically than it sounds. 1/2" thick, at least 24" × 68". NBR or TPE foam. A thin yoga mat is not enough padding for floor core work.
4 Digital kitchen scale
search →		 $10–15 You cannot hit 2,800 calories and 150 g of protein reliably by eyeballing. Two weeks with a scale calibrates your estimates permanently. Grams and ounces, tare function, 11 lb capacity. Any cheap one works.
5 Bathroom scale
search →		 $15–25 Daily weigh-ins are the primary feedback loop for whether you're eating enough. 0.2 lb increments. Ignore the "body fat %" feature on cheap scales — bioimpedance from feet only is close to useless.

search →		 $6–10 Monthly measurements catch progress the scale hides. See the measurement protocol. A flexible cloth or fiberglass tape with inches. The spring-loaded "body tape" versions that apply constant tension are worth the extra couple of dollars for consistency.

Choosing a pull-up bar

TypePriceProsConsVerdict
Leverage doorway bar
Hooks over the frame, uses your weight as the anchor		 $25–40 No drilling. Removable. Multiple grips. Doubles as a dip/push-up station on the floor. Reliable when installed correctly. Needs a door frame with proper trim and a wall above it. Can mark or dent soft trim — use the foam pads or a folded towel. Limited headroom means you may need to bend your knees. Buy this one.
Pressure-mount / tension bar
Screws outward against the door jamb		 $20–30 Cheapest. Sits inside the frame. Can slip and drop you. Relies entirely on friction and correct tightening, and it loosens over time. Only one grip position. Avoid.
Wall or ceiling mounted $40–90 Rock solid. Full headroom for leg raises and muscle-ups. Highest weight rating. Permanent. Requires drilling into studs or joists and a landlord who won't mind. Best if you own the place.
Free-standing power tower $120–250 Pull-ups, dips, and knee raises in one unit. No mounting at all. Big footprint (~4×4 ft). Cheap ones wobble alarmingly. Heavy to move. Great if you have the space and budget.
Park bar / playground Free Free, full range, usually has dip bars and rings nearby, and training outside is genuinely good for adherence. Weather. Travel time. Not always available at 6 am. Use it if it's close.
Before you hang from anything, check three things. (1) The door frame: the trim must be solidly attached to the framing behind it, not just tacked to drywall. Push hard on it. (2) The weight rating must exceed your bodyweight with a wide margin — 300 lb minimum at 150 lb, because dynamic loading during a rep can be several times your static weight. (3) Test it slowly: hang with your feet still on the ground and gradually shift weight onto it before you commit. Do this test every time you reinstall the bar. And keep the floor under it clear.

Tier 2 — months 3–6, once you've earned it

Buy these when the program actually calls for them, not up front. Nothing here is necessary before month 3.

ItemPriceUnlocksWhat to look for
Parallettes
search →		 $30–60 L-sits, deficit push-ups, tuck planche work, handstand work on a neutral wrist, and dips if they're tall enough. Extremely versatile for the price. Low ones (6–8") for L-sits and planche work; tall ones (12–24") if you also want dips. Wooden dowels are kinder on the hands than steel. Check they don't tip sideways.
Gymnastic rings
search →		 $30–50 Ring rows, ring dips, ring push-ups, support holds, and eventually the front lever and muscle-up. The single most versatile piece of equipment in calisthenics. 1.1" wood rings with long numbered straps and cam buckles. Wood grips better and is warmer than plastic. You need somewhere to hang them: a pull-up bar, a tree branch, a beam, or a park frame.
Dip station / parallel bars
search →		 $60–120 Real dips (much better than bench dips), L-sits, and support holds. Adjustable height, wide stable base, 400+ lb rating. Set the bars just wider than your shoulders. Some doorway bars convert into a floor dip station — check before buying separately.
Weighted vest or dip belt
search →		 $40–120 The cleanest way to progress once you can do 10+ pull-ups or 15+ dips. Beats hunting for ever more exotic variations. Adjustable in small increments (2.5–5 lb). A dip belt plus plates is cheaper and more precise; a vest is more convenient and works for push-ups and squats too. A backpack with books does 80% of this for free.
Ab wheel
search →		 $12–25 One of the best anti-extension core exercises there is, and very cheap. Wide wheel or dual wheels for stability. Start from the knees with a short range — it is much harder than it looks.
Door anchor for bands
search →		 $8–15 Face pulls, Pallof presses, band rows, and external rotations at any angle. Foam ball or thick padded type so it can't pull through the door.
Furniture sliders
search →		 $8–15 Sliding leg curls, slide-out planks, reverse lunges — works on carpet, where towels don't. Get the double-sided kind (hard side for carpet, felt side for hard floors).

What to skip entirely

ItemWhy not
Pressure-mount tension pull-up barsGenuine fall risk. They rely on friction and loosen over time. Not worth saving $10.
"Push-up boards" with color-coded holesSolves a problem you don't have. Hand position isn't what's limiting you.
Ab belts, vibrating plates, EMS devicesDo nothing. There is no passive route to muscle.
Grip strengtheners (spring type)Dead hangs and pull-ups build far more usable grip than a spring gripper.
Weight-loss teas, waist trainers, sauna suitsWater loss theater.

Barefoot-style "training" shoes, day oneYou're training on the floor. Go barefoot or wear socks. Buy shoes when you have a reason.

Clothing & shoes

  • Train barefoot or in socks for indoor calisthenics. Better proprioception, and for pistol squats and handstands a flat foot on the ground is what you want. Running shoes with a soft, elevated heel are actively unhelpful for squatting.
  • If you want shoes: flat, thin, firm sole — trainers, court shoes, or minimalist shoes. Not running shoes.
  • Fitted shirts or none. A loose shirt gets caught under your hands during push-ups and hides the form issues you're trying to see on video.
  • Shorts you can squat deeply in without restriction.
  • Long sleeves for bar work in cold weather — cold tissue is stiffer and more injury-prone.

The free option: use a park

Most public parks and school playgrounds have a pull-up bar, and many US cities now have dedicated calisthenics or "fitness court" setups with bars at multiple heights, parallel bars, and sometimes rings. That is a complete gym for $0. Search for "calisthenics park near me" or check the map at calisthenics-parks.com, a community-maintained worldwide directory.

Practical notes: bring liquid chalk if the bars are slick, wear gloves or long sleeves in winter because cold steel is genuinely painful, go early or late if you're self-conscious (nobody is watching, but it takes a few weeks to believe that), and have an indoor fallback session for weather so the habit never breaks.

Three sample budgets

$0 — the honest minimum

Floor, chairs, table, towel, backpack full of books, and a nearby park bar if you have one.

Gets you: all of Phase 1 and most of Phase 2. Genuinely enough for 3–6 months of real progress. The gap is vertical pulling, which the park bar closes entirely.

$60 — the smart start

Doorway pull-up bar ($30) + resistance band set ($25) + tape measure ($8).

Gets you: every pattern in the program, properly, through month 6. If you buy one thing on this page, buy this combination.

$250 — the full home setup

Tier 1 in full ($130) + rings ($40) + parallettes ($45) + ab wheel ($20) + door anchor ($12).

Gets you: two to three years of progression without ever needing a gym, including rings work, L-sits, front lever training, and muscle-up practice.
