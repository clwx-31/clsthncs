Rung finder

Which rung are you on?

Eleven questions, about two minutes. For each movement pattern, pick the hardest version you can currently do for at least 5 but no more than about 15 clean, full-range reps — that's the version you should be training. Your answers save to this browser and drive the session runner.

Two rules that make this easy. If you can already do more than 15 clean reps of something, it's too easy — pick the next one up. If you can't do 5 with full range and control, it's too hard — pick the one below. Being one rung too easy costs you a week. Being one rung too hard costs you a month of half-reps and sore joints, so when you're between two, choose the easier one.

Your rungs

How to use these

  • Train the rung, don't skip ahead. The rep gate on each rung isn't about your muscles — those are ready long before your tendons are. It buys the connective-tissue adaptation that prevents month-four elbow and shoulder problems. Why →
  • Move up when every set hits the top of the range with clean form and 1–2 reps left in the tank. Then reset to the bottom of the range on the new rung. That's double progression, and it's the whole progression mechanism.
  • Expect different rungs on different patterns. Almost everyone is further along on pushing than pulling, and further along on both than on legs. That's normal, not a flaw.
  • Redo this whenever something feels off — after a layoff, after an injury, or when an exercise stops feeling hard. It takes two minutes.
